### 80,000 Hours — We can guess what intergalactic war would look like. And strangely, it matters.
*Published 2026-06-18 · 15m · [Listen](https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/war-in-space/?utm_campaign=podcast__space-war&utm_source=80000+Hours+Podcast&utm_medium=podcast)*

**Guest / Format:** Rob Wiblin — solo explainer, drawing on analysis by researcher Beren Millidge

**One-sentence framing:** Using known physics alone, we can identify the only three viable weapons for intergalactic war — and the analysis suggests defenders have an overwhelming structural advantage, meaning violence doesn't pay at galactic scales and humanity's near-term choices about cosmic expansion may matter more than expected.

**Topics**
- **Setup: Kardashev III civilisations**
  - Two K3 civilisations — each harnessing every star in their home galaxy — positioned adjacent galaxies; no FTL, no wormholes; technology maxed out
- **Weapon 1: Lasers**
  - K3 civilisations could potentially coordinate Dyson spheres across an entire galaxy to fire synchronised lasers at a planet-sized target 2.5 million light-years away; kinetic energy delivered ~10^35 watts, vastly exceeding Earth's gravitational binding energy (~10^32 joules)
  - Fatal flaw: no course correction. Round-trip information lag of 5 million years means lasers can only hit fixed targets (stars, Dyson spheres, planets on predictable orbits); anything on a random orbit is effectively immune
- **Weapon 2: Relativistic Kill Vehicles (RKVs)**
  - Physical objects accelerated to a large fraction of the speed of light; carry onboard sensors and steering, so can update targeting in the final thousands of years of flight
  - Defender advantage: deflecting an RKV requires vastly less energy than launching one — a small lateral nudge is sufficient because space is almost entirely empty; defensive laser installations can melt RKV surfaces and apply that nudge
- **Weapon 3: Invasion fleets**
  - Ships that cross the void and slow down to establish a beachhead; most disadvantaged approach because they're slower than light and beams can be used against them on approach
- **The defender's surprising advantage**
  - Across all three weapon classes, the defense is cheaper than the offense; violence doesn't pay at intergalactic scales
  - Universe likely settles into a stable patchwork — each galaxy belongs to whoever colonised it first
- **Why this matters now**
  - If the patchwork model is correct, what humanity does in the next few centuries — which galaxies we reach and seed first — could permanently determine what fraction of the accessible cosmos belongs to Earth-originating life
  - Whether our existence turns out to be a net positive or negative for the universe becomes a live question

**Notable claims / arguments**
- A coordinated intergalactic laser from K3 civilisation could deliver 10^35 watts to any fixed target — 1,000 times the gravitational binding energy of Earth — but cannot hit moving targets
- The cost asymmetry between offense and defense at intergalactic distances is so extreme that rational actors would not initiate war
- The episode was recorded March 2, 2026

**Frameworks / mental models introduced**
- Kardashev scale (K1/K2/K3) — civilisations classified by total energy use
- Defender's advantage — the principle that in a given conflict environment, defense is cheaper than offense

**References (books, papers, people, prior episodes)**
- Beren Millidge — AI researcher whose analysis of intergalactic warfare physics is the basis for this video

**Quotes (if transcript available)**
> "The universe is just unfathomably empty. And that emptiness is a huge asset to any defender." — Rob Wiblin

> "What humanity does over the next few centuries could permanently decide which slice of the cosmos belongs to Earth-originating life — and whether our very existence turns out to be a good thing, or a bad one." — Rob Wiblin

**One-sentence framing:** An introduction to Ludwig Klages' vitalist philosophy — his magnum opus *Der Geist als Widersacher der Seele* — and what it means to see "Spirit" as the enemy of the living Soul.

  - Late 19th/early 20th-century German philosopher, psychologist, graphologist; associated with Stefan George Circle and later broke with it
  - Wrote *Der Geist als Widersacher der Seele* (The Spirit as Adversary of the Soul) — a massive, difficult work defending the primacy of life and "soul" over rational "spirit"
- **The central argument: Spirit vs Soul**
  - Klages distinguishes between Seele (soul) — the living, biocentric, cosmic principle — and Geist (spirit) — the rational, abstracting, "world-historical" principle that Klages sees as parasitic on and destructive of life
  - Modern civilisation, technology, and abstraction are symptoms of Geist's domination
- **Vitalism and the philosophy of life (Lebensphilosophie)**
  - Klages as part of the German Lebensphilosophie tradition (alongside Bergson, Nietzsche, Dilthey)
  - Paul Bishop's framing: Klages offers a "vitalist toolkit" for rethinking modernity

### Odd Lots — The Iran War's Lasting Scars Across Asia
*Published 2026-06-16 · 20m · [Listen](https://omny.fm/shows/odd-lots/the-iran-war-s-lasting-scars-across-asia)*

**Guest / Format:** Oanh Ha (Bloomberg reporter) with Tracy Alloway and Joe Weisenthal — panel

**One-sentence framing:** Even with a US-Iran deal reopening the Strait of Hormuz, the conflict has forced durable structural shifts in Asia's food and energy supply chains that won't reverse quickly.

**Topics**
- **The asymmetry of burden-sharing**
  - Wealthy countries (US, Japan, Hong Kong) have drained strategic petroleum reserves and used subsidies to cushion prices; poorer Asian nations have been forced into demand destruction — India discouraging flights, China cutting oil demand by ~1.5 million barrels/day (~9%)
  - Diesel prices in Hong Kong up ~50% since the Iran War began; gasoline at HK$33/litre (~$16/gallon US equivalent)
- **Food stress building in Southeast Asia**
  - Closure of the Strait of Hormuz will create a food stress during the next planting season; if food stress hits consumers, demand for Chinese manufactured exports may fall, compounding China's economic pressure

**Topics**

  - Distinct from traditional diversification; asks "what happens to this portfolio when things break?" rather than "what's the Sharpe ratio?"
- **Portfolio construction in an unstable world**
  - Why many traditional frameworks are no longer fit for purpose; what the alternatives look like
