### Odd Lots — Gita Gopinath on Why Interest Rates Have Surged All Around the World
*Published 2026-05-29 · 51min · [Listen](https://omny.fm/shows/odd-lots/gita-gopinath-on-why-interest-rates-have-surged-all-around-the-world)*

**Guest / Format:** Interview — Gita Gopinath (Harvard economics professor; former First Deputy Managing Director, IMF) with hosts Tracy Alloway and Joe Weisenthal

**One-sentence framing:** The global bond sell-off is not a transitory oil story but a structural repricing driven by higher r-star, fiscal deterioration, and AI's insatiable capital appetite — and governments may not have the backstop capacity they once did.

**Topics**
- **The global bond sell-off**
  - UK gilt long end hit highest level since 1998; US 10-year treasury yield approaching 5%
  - Yields have been moving partly in line with the oil price, but something more secular appears to be underway
  - Torsten Slock's argument: AI "FOMO" among companies means higher rates don't slow capex the way they normally would — missing the frontier model is a worse outcome than expensive financing
- **r-star has drifted higher**
  - Pre-pandemic r-star was around 0%; now Gopinath places it around 1 percentage point
  - Three factors: inflation expectations ratcheted up; large US deficits with no near-term path to consolidation; AI capex boom crowding out sovereign bond demand
  - Gopinath: "we are at the end of secular stagnation"
- **Public debt fragility**
  - Gopinath's framing: bond markets are "in a fragile place" — confluence of demographics, high debt levels, and AI capital intensity all pulling in the same inflationary direction
  - Risk that governments will lack the fiscal space to backstop markets in the next major shock
- **AI and interest rates**
  - Capital needs of the AI build-out represent a secular shift in investment demand, not a cyclical blip
  - This crowds out sovereign bonds and contributes to the term premium

**Notable claims / arguments**
- The most worrisome thing in markets right now is not tariffs or geopolitics but the level and trajectory of public debt globally
- Secular stagnation — the regime of chronically insufficient investment — is definitively over
- Investors may be wrong to assume that central banks and governments will backstop markets as they did in 2008 and 2020, because fiscal capacity has been used up

**Frameworks / mental models introduced**
- r-star (neutral real interest rate) — the interest rate consistent with stable inflation at full employment; Gopinath argues this has shifted up by ~1 percentage point since pre-pandemic

**References (books, papers, people, prior episodes)**
- Torsten Slock — chart on AI FOMO and rate insensitivity of capex

**Quotes (if transcript available)**
> "I actually do think the one that's most worrisome is what we see with public debt levels everywhere in the world." — Gita Gopinath, ~4:12

> "We've suddenly moved away from the pre-pandemic period of low for long interest rates… I think we are at the end of secular stagnation at this point." — Gita Gopinath, ~5:07

**Open questions raised**
- If a major shock hits and governments lack fiscal space, what does the shock-absorption mechanism look like?
- Is the AI capex boom a one-time infrastructure build that eventually resolves, or does it sustain elevated rates indefinitely?

### 80,000 Hours Podcast — What makes for a dream job? | Benjamin Todd
*Published 2026-05-28 · 28min · [Listen](https://80000hours.org/career-guide/dream-job/)*

**Guest / Format:** Solo narration — Benjamin Todd (co-founder, 80,000 Hours), reading Chapter 1 of his new book

**One-sentence framing:** "Follow your passion" is empirically weak career advice; the research points instead to five ingredients of job satisfaction that center on meaningful contribution and skill development rather than pre-existing interest.

**Topics**
- **Why "follow your passion" fails**
  - A meta-analysis of over 100 studies: correlation between interest-job fit and job satisfaction is only 0.19
  - 90% of Canadian students identified music, art, or sport as their greatest passion; under 3% of Canadian jobs are in those fields — so even if 1-in-10 follow their passion, most will fail
  - Passion is not stable: interests change significantly over five-year horizons, making them a poor planning basis
  - Following passion can cause people to prematurely eliminate careers that don't feel rewarding from day one, even though most careers require time to become engaging
- **What actually predicts job satisfaction**
  - High income matters less than popular belief; low stress is actually counterproductive (senior leaders of difficult projects report lower stress than the people they manage)
  - The five key ingredients from the 80k Hours research: work that is engaging in itself, work that helps others, ethical surroundings, supportive colleagues, and work that fits your life
- **Don't follow your passion — do what matters**
  - The alternative framing: get good at something that genuinely helps others; passion tends to follow mastery and meaning rather than precede them

**Notable claims / arguments**
- Senior leaders of difficult projects have lower stress than the people they manage — suggesting that challenge and responsibility are compatible with wellbeing
- Passion for a field is not reliably predictive of job satisfaction in that field; the correlation is surprisingly weak
- The most competitive job markets (creative fields, sports) are exactly the ones that passion directs people toward, lowering their expected return

**Frameworks / mental models introduced**
- Five ingredients of a dream job — engagement, helping others, ethics, colleagues, life fit — derived from a review of decades of research

**References (books, papers, people, prior episodes)**
- 80,000 Hours: How to Have a Fulfilling Career that Does Good — Benjamin Todd — the book this chapter is drawn from (published May 26, 2026)

**Quotes (if transcript available)**
> "A meta-analysis of over 100 studies found that the correlation between interest, job fit, and job satisfaction is only about 0.19, so really pretty low." — Benjamin Todd (via Rob Wiblin intro), ~0:02:30

> "If you're passionate about literature, it's easy to think that you must become a writer to have a satisfying career. But in fact, there are probably many other jobs that could satisfy you, so long as they're fulfilling in other ways." — Benjamin Todd, ~15:54

**Open questions raised**
- If passion follows mastery rather than predicting it, how should someone choose *which* skills to develop in the first place?

### Dr John Vervaeke — William Desmond and John Vervaeke: Strong Transcendence, Plato, and the Between
*Published 2026-05-28 · 1h 37min · [Listen](https://d0785b51-c8bc-4851-a061-e7c5db34338f.libsyn.com/william-desmond-and-john-vervaeke-strong-transcendence-plato-and-the-between)*

**Guest / Format:** Dialogue — William Desmond (philosopher) and John Vervaeke

**One-sentence framing:** Desmond and Vervaeke argue that Plato's concept of the metaxu — the "between" — provides a philosophically rigorous alternative to modernity's flat ontology and buffered self, grounding a participatory understanding of transcendence that is both epistemically and ontologically significant.

**Topics**
- **Strong vs. psychological transcendence**
  - Modernity treats transcendence as a superior psychological state; Vervaeke argues for a stronger sense in which transcendence discloses truths otherwise unavailable and changes the knower's relation to reality
  - This challenges flat ontology, representational cognition, and the fact-value split
- **Plato as philosopher of the between**
  - Desmond presents Plato not as a dry two-worlds theorist but as a thinker of mimesis, eros, mania, and dialogue
  - The dialogical form is not ornamental: the drama, characters, and movement are integral to the philosophy
  - Eros and mania mark two directions of transcendence: from below upward and from above downward
- **The metaxu and modernity's dichotomies**
  - The metaxu (between) names a porous relational space before, beneath, and beyond modern dichotomies (subject/object, fact/value, immanent/transcendent)
  - Modernity's fact-value split risks producing default atheism or nihilism
  - The buffered self — modern interiority sealed off from the world — forecloses participatory knowing
- **Deep memory, possibility, and God**
  - Deep memory as ontological depth, not mere storage; a source of imagination and contact with what enables intelligibility
  - "Living possibility" versus logical possibility: possibility as enabling power, not mere counterfactual
  - Nicholas of Cusa, Daoism, wu wei, and theoria as pilgrimage emerge in the later conversation
- **Intercultural philosophy**
  - Both thinkers converge on needing a philosophical "Silk Road" beyond Western frameworks
  - Daoist midwifery (wu wei, giving way) compared to Socratic midwifery

**Notable claims / arguments**
- The dialogue form of Plato cannot be stripped to extract arguments — the drama is the philosophy
- Mimesis is not imitation in a debased sense but a relation between image and original that preserves their difference
- Pilgrimage and theoria are linked: philosophical transformation requires being underway, not observing from nowhere

**Frameworks / mental models introduced**
- Metaxology — Desmond's philosophical system centered on the between as prior to modern subject-object dichotomies
- Transjectivity — Vervaeke's term for how relevance realization is grounded in participatory relation rather than internal representation
- Anagoge — the upward turning of the soul; periagoge in Plato's cave parable

**References (books, papers, people, prior episodes)**
- William Desmond — Being and the Between; Ethics and the Between; God and the Between; Art, Origins, Otherness
- Plato — Symposium, Ion, Sophist, Republic, Laches
- Plotinus and Proclus

- Thomas Aquinas

**Open questions raised**
- Can participatory and dialogical philosophical practice survive translation into academic publication format, or does the medium inevitably undercut the message?
- What is the relationship between AI-outsourced memory and the ontological depth Desmond locates in deep memory?

### Odd Lots — Brendan Greeley on the Real 500-Year History of the Dollar
*Published 2026-05-28 · 55min · [Listen](https://omny.fm/shows/odd-lots/brendan-greeley-on-the-real-500-year-history-of-the-dollar)*

**Guest / Format:** Interview — Brendan Greeley (author, The Almighty Dollar; contributing editor, Financial Times) with Tracy Alloway and Joe Weisenthal

**One-sentence framing:** The dollar is 500 years old, predates the United States, and derives from a German word for a Spanish coin minted from Mexican silver — understanding this history reveals what actually backs the dollar and who really controls it.

**Topics**
- **The dollar's surprising etymology**
  - "Dollar" derives from "Thaler," a German word applied to a Spanish peso minted from silver found in Mexico
  - The dollar as a concept long predates the US as a nation-state
- **Flavors of the dollar**
  - Cash dollars, bank dollars (created by commercial banks, never physically seen), eurodollars (held in non-US institutions), and stablecoins — all trade at 1:1 to each other but are institutionally distinct
  - Mark Carney's Jackson Hole speech on America's declining share of global GDP vs. the dollar's growing share of global trade — the misalignment as a source of structural tension
- **What actually backs the dollar**
  - Not gold; not merely US military power; Greeley's book traces the specific institutional and commercial infrastructure that gives the dollar its purchasing power across 500 years
- **The future of the dollar**
  - Understanding the origin is prerequisite to understanding whether the dollar can be displaced and by what

**Notable claims / arguments**
- The dollar's longevity has more to do with deep institutional infrastructure than with any single policy choice — making it more resilient to displacement than critics suggest
- The "exorbitant privilege" framing misses that dollar dominance is also a burden — the Fed sets rates for American conditions but those rates transmit globally

**References (books, papers, people, prior episodes)**
- The Almighty Dollar: 500 Years of the World's Most Powerful Money — Brendan Greeley
- Mark Carney — Jackson Hole speech on dollar's role vs. US share of global GDP

**Quotes (if transcript available)**
> "Like which came first? The money? I love like I love philosophy of money. That's sort of like history of money." — Joe Weisenthal, ~1:03

**Open questions raised**
- If stablecoins increasingly intermediate dollar transactions, does the US retain effective monetary sovereignty even if nominal dollarization continues?

  - His scale (Close Encounter of the first, second, third kind) became the standard taxonomy

  - His research suggested the phenomenon has been consistent across cultures and centuries — undermining the "spacecraft from other planets" frame
- **Rudolf Steiner and esoteric epistemology**
  - Steiner's approach to knowledge — bridging scientific rigor and spiritual perception — provides French with a framework for taking UAP contact seriously without abandoning critical standards
  - Anthroposophy's non-dualist ontology allows for entities that are real but not material in the conventional sense
- **Aaron French's research: "Knowing Others" paper**
  - French's paper (Correspondences journal) examines how the UAP phenomenon challenges standard epistemologies of other-knowing

**Notable claims / arguments**
- The consistency of UAP contact reports across cultures and centuries suggests the phenomenon is not reducible to modern Cold War-era misidentification
- Vallee's interdimensional hypothesis remains more parsimonious than the ETH given the physics of interstellar travel

**References (books, papers, people, prior episodes)**
- J. Allen Hynek — Project Blue Book; the CE taxonomy
- Jacques Vallee — interdimensional hypothesis
- Rudolf Steiner — Anthroposophy
